# Inversion Lens

**Type:** Lens  
**Rarity:** Uncommon  
**Purpose:** Reframe problems by inverting assumptions

---

## The Premise

Every problem has an assumed direction. Most agents think forward. This lens forces you backward — invert the premise, flip the constraint, reverse the goal.

## The Questions

Apply these four inversions to any problem:

### 1. Constraint Inversion
**Original:** "How do I MAXIMIZE X?"  
**Inverted:** "How do I MINIMIZE X?"  
**New view:** What if the goal is the opposite? Can removing X reveal something?

### 2. Subject-Object Inversion  
**Original:** "How do I affect Y?"  
**Inverted:** "How does Y affect me?"  
**New view:** Who has agency here? Am I the cause or the effect?

### 3. Time Inversion
**Original:** "How do I get to Z?"  
**Inverted:** "How did Z get to me?"  
**New view:** Trace backward. What's the origin? Can I intervene earlier?

### 4. Value Inversion
**Original:** "This is GOOD."  
**Inverted:** "How could this be BAD?"  
**New view:** What would have to be true for this to fail?

## Application Protocol

1. State the original problem clearly
2. Apply all four inversions
3. For each inversion, identify: what NEW problem emerges?
4. Select the most insightful inversion
5. Solve THAT problem
6. Translate the solution back to the original framing

## Example

**Original:** "How do I increase sales?"  
**Inversion:** "How do I decrease sales?"  
**Insight:** By identifying what kills sales, I learn what promotes them.  
**New approach:** Remove the blockers, sales increase naturally.

## Quality Gate

A valid inversion lens output must include:
- At least 2 different inversion types applied
- At least 1 insight that wouldn't emerge from direct analysis
- A connection back to the original problem

---

## Why This Works

Inversion is a first-principles move. When you invert, you remove the assumption layer and see the mechanism underneath. Problems that seem complex often have simple inversions.
