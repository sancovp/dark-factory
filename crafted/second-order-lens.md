# Lens: Second-Order Thinking
Type: Lens
A reusable analytical lens that forces consideration of consequences of consequences.

## Application
Apply this lens BEFORE reaching conclusions. It transforms surface-level analysis into systemic thinking.

## The Questions
When examining any claim, decision, or plan, ask:

1. **Immediate Effect**: What happens directly?
2. **First-Order Response**: How do people/systems react to that?
3. **Second-Order Response**: How do people react to those reactions?
4. **Equilibrium**: Where does this stabilize? Is that desirable?
5. **Unintended Consequences**: What's the worst plausible second-order outcome?

## Example Transformation
**Before Second-Order Lens:**
"Raising prices increases revenue."

**After Second-Order Lens:**
"Raising prices increases revenue immediately, but customers may switch to competitors (first-order), who then invest savings in better products (second-order), eventually making our offering obsolete at equilibrium."

## When to Apply
- Before committing to any strategic decision
- When evaluating others' proposals
- During risk assessment
- At the START of analysis, not the end

## Quality Indicator
If your second-order analysis doesn't change your conclusion, you're not looking deep enough. The lens works best when it REVERSES first-order intuition.
