# Recipe: Causation → Second-Order Pipeline
Type: Recipe
Output Type: Rare
Yield: 1 composite analysis pipeline that traces causal mechanisms through multiple consequence layers

## Ingredients
1. Lens: Causation Lens (Rare) — transforms correlations into causal hypotheses
2. Lens: Second-Order Thinking (Rare) — traces consequences through cascading reactions

## Assembly
1. **Input**: Any correlation or co-occurrence claim ("X relates to Y")
2. **Stage 1 — Apply Causation Lens:**
   - Ask: "What causal mechanism could produce this pattern?"
   - Ask: "What changed to make this correlation visible?"
   - Output: Mechanism hypothesis + confidence level (correlational/circumstantial/strong)
3. **Stage 2 — Apply Second-Order Lens to Stage 1 Output:**
   - Ask: "What happens directly from this mechanism?"
   - Ask: "How do systems/people react to those effects?"
   - Ask: "How do people react to THOSE reactions?"
   - Ask: "Where does this stabilize?"
4. **Stage 3 — Synthesize:**
   - Combine Stage 1 mechanism with Stage 2 cascade
   - Identify: Which second-order effects could feed back into the original mechanism?
   - Label: self-reinforcing, self-undermining, or neutral cascade

## Quality Check
- Remove Causation Lens: Can you derive mechanism hypotheses from second-order alone? (Must: no)
- Remove Second-Order: Can you trace cascades from mechanism alone? (Must: no)
- Composition creates value: pipeline output > sum of separate lens applications

## Expected Rarity
Two Rare lenses → Rare pipeline (composed utility exceeds component utility)

## Why This Recipe Works
Causation without second-order gives you mechanism without trajectory. Second-order without causation gives you cascade without origin. Together: trace a cause through time's ripple effects, forming hypotheses that account for systemic feedback.
